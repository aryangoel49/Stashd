Three steps, and two of them are one key.

Stashd lives in your menu bar. It doesn't change how you work. It just remembers it for you.

1

Stash

Press ⌃S. Stashd looks at what's open and ticks only what belongs to the project you're on. Add a note for later if you like.

2

Pack up

The windows fly into the menu bar and the folder goes to sleep. Your screen is clear. Unsaved work still asks to be saved first.

3

Unpack

Open the folder when you're back. A short brief says where you stopped, and one click brings everything back.

It knows what belongs together.

The sorting runs on your Mac, in an instant. No account, nothing to set up.

One project, not every tab

Writing an essay with a shoe sale open on the side? Only the essay goes in. Stashd reads what your tabs are about, so "Carbon tax" and "Paris Agreement" land together even with no words in common.

A brief that says where you stopped

Each folder gets a short note: what you were on, what's done, and what's next.

Your work stays on your Mac.

There's no account and no server. Folders are plain files on your computer.

Sorting happens on your Mac. A small model runs locally to tell which tabs belong together.

It never reads inside your files. Only names, tab titles and addresses.

Briefs are optional. Use Apple Intelligence on your Mac, or connect your own Claude key.

Private windows are left alone. Incognito tabs are never saved or closed.

Stash it. Close the laptop.

Free for Mac. Takes a minute to set up.
